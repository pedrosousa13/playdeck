# Live

Playdeck reports whether the active source is live on `PlayerState.live`, and
offers one command to catch up to its edge, `seekToLiveEdge`. The two are
gated separately: knowing a source is live and being able to seek to its edge
are different facts, and a provider can have the first without the second.
`capabilities.liveEdge` says whether the second exists per source, and
`Player.LiveIndicator` is the one part built over both — see the **Contract**
guide's `live` entry for its data attributes, and the **Capabilities matrix**
guide for the general capability-gating shape this bends.

## The state

Two things to read, and they answer different questions:

- **`live`**, a `PlayerLiveState`: `null` while the source is not live or its
  liveness is not yet known, otherwise `{ isLive, atLiveEdge, offsetFromEdge }`.
  `isLive` is `true` whenever the value is non-null — the shared derivation
  never publishes `isLive: false`, so there is no third, reachable "known not
  live" case beyond `null` itself. `atLiveEdge` marks playback within ten
  seconds of the provider's own edge, the tolerance every adapter measures
  against. `offsetFromEdge` is how far behind that edge playback has fallen,
  in whole seconds and never negative — `0` at or ahead of it. It is rounded
  deliberately: an unrounded float would differ on nearly every `timeupdate`,
  and whole seconds is what `Player.Time` renders at besides.
- **`capabilities.liveEdge`**, an `Availability`: whether the provider can
  report a live edge to seek to at all, told apart the way `chapters` is —
  `unavailable`/`provider` means the provider has no way to answer this for
  any source, `unavailable`/`source` means this particular source is not
  live.

The two are independent facts. A source can be known live (`live` non-null)
while there is nothing to seek to — see Wistia below.

## Which providers can report it

| Provider                   | `live`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | `liveEdge`                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| native, native-HLS engines | Derived from the element's own raw `duration` and moving `seekable` window                                                                                                                                                                   | `available` once live and the seekable window has a finite end; `unavailable`/`source` otherwise                             |
| hls.js engine              | The same derivation, with hls.js's own live flag taken as an authoritative hint over the raw duration                                                                                                                                        | `available` once hls.js reports a finite `liveSyncPosition` over a live, scrubbable window; `unavailable`/`source` otherwise |
| vimeo                      | Never populated — `@vimeo/player` has no live concept anywhere in its type definitions or its shipped code, so `live` stays `null` on a live-shaped embed                                                                                    | Fixed `unavailable`/`provider`                                                                                               |
| youtube                    | Never populated — the adapter recognises `/live/<id>`, the canonical URL for a broadcast, at the source-detection stage (**Provider setup**), but reports nothing about liveness once attached, so `live` stays `null` for the whole session | Fixed `unavailable`/`provider`                                                                                               |
| wistia                     | Derived from the embed's own `mediaData.mediaType`, `"LiveStream"` being the one live value; `duration()` stands in for the edge, since `PublicApi` has no seekable-range accessor                                                           | Fixed `unavailable`/`provider`                                                                                               |

Wistia is the instructive middle case: it can tell you the stream is live and
how far behind its own notion of the edge playback is, and still cannot
honour `seekToLiveEdge` — `PublicApi` gives this adapter neither a seekable
range nor a dedicated live-edge member to build a real target from.
`Player.LiveIndicator` renders there as a permanently disabled badge, never a
button.

## `seekToLiveEdge`

One command, issued through `controller.seekToLiveEdge()` directly, or
through `Player.LiveIndicator`'s own click handler. It is gated ahead of
dispatch rather than left to fail once issued: unlike every other command on
`PlayerController`, there is no controller-computed fallback position, so a
provider that cannot answer is refused before ever being asked —
`capabilities.liveEdge` not `available`, or no `seekToLiveEdge` method on the
active provider, both refuse the same way.

Where it lands is the provider's own notion of the edge, never one the
controller computes: hls.js's `liveSyncPosition`, deliberately behind the raw
seekable end, on the hls.js engine; the raw seekable end itself — the only
notion of an edge a plain media element has — on the native and native-HLS
engines. hls.js also withholds `liveEdge` while its seekable window is too
narrow to be worth scrubbing — the same width check that, on a narrow live
window, takes `seek` itself back to `unavailable`/`source`.

## `Player.LiveIndicator`

Unlike every other capability-gated part in this package, `LiveIndicator`
does not vanish when its gating capability is unavailable — a deliberate,
named exception, because it reports a property of the stream (`live`) rather
than offering a control (`seekToLiveEdge`), and being live is true whether or
not a seek-to-edge command exists on the active provider:

- Renders nothing while `live` is `null`.
- Otherwise always renders `data-playdeck-part="live"`, with `data-state`
  `"at-edge"` or `"behind-edge"` from `live.atLiveEdge`.
- `disabled` reflects `capabilities.liveEdge` rather than `live` — set
  whenever `liveEdge` is not `available`, so Wistia's badge stays permanently
  non-interactive while the same badge over a native or hls.js source becomes
  a real button once `liveEdge` resolves.
- Clicking it issues `controller.seekToLiveEdge()`, only where it is not
  `disabled`.
- The default `aria-label` is `"Go to live"` only where pressing it would act
  — `liveEdge` seekable and behind the edge — and `"Live"` in every other
  reachable state, including at the edge with seeking available. A consumer
  `aria-label` always wins, in every state.

It renders no other `data-playdeck-part`: `live` is the whole of its own
contract, documented alongside every other part's in the **Contract** guide.

## The control bar on a live source

`Player.SeekSlider` and `Player.Time` both take a live source with no special
prop for it, and each reads a different slice of state to do it.

`SeekSlider` never reads `live` at all — it infers a scrubbable window purely
from `duration` and `seekable`: `[0, duration]` for a source with one, or the
extent of `seekable` where there is none, which is what a live source with a
moving seekable window publishes instead. Where neither exists yet — a live stream
reporting no seekable window at all — there is nothing to scrub, and the
slider carries `data-state="idle"` rather than a range that cannot move; the
**Contract** guide's own styling example for the part covers exactly that
state.

`Player.Time` does read `live` directly, and reports something different for
`type="current"` on a live source: not elapsed time, but
`live.offsetFromEdge` — how far behind the edge playback is — displaying the
word `LIVE` (overridable through the `liveLabel` prop) once `live.atLiveEdge`,
or a negative offset (`-0:42`) while behind it. `type="duration"` and
`type="remaining"` have no total to report on a live source, since its
`duration` is never a finite number, and display no text of their own — the
untimed `<span>` the **Contract** guide's `time` entry documents, rather than
a `0:00` that would misstate a live stream as a zero-length video.

## Accessibility

`Player.LiveIndicator`'s trigger is a real `<button>` in both its states,
`disabled` rather than `aria-disabled` where `liveEdge` is unavailable — out
of the tab order and announced unavailable rather than merely styled that
way. Its label follows the rule above, and satisfies WCAG 2.5.3 _Label in
Name_ the same way every other stateful control in this package does: the
visible text stays `"Live"` throughout, and `"Go to live"` contains it.

## Out of scope

A standalone live-edge button, outside `LiveIndicator`, does not exist —
`seekToLiveEdge` is issued through its click handler or through
`controller.seekToLiveEdge()` directly, with no dedicated menu or slider
affordance of its own. Markers on the seek slider for the live edge, or for
how much of the seekable window is scrubbable versus already lost, do not
exist either; a consumer composes those over `Player.SeekSlider`'s own
`seekable`-derived geometry.
